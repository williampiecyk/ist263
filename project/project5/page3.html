Info

The Pantheon, located in Rome, is one of the best-preserved buildings from ancient Rome. The structure standing today was commissioned by Emperor Hadrian and built around 113-125 AD, replacing an earlier temple originally constructed by Marcus Agrippa around 27 BC (whose inscription Hadrian preserved on the façade, leading to centuries of confusion over who actually built it).

The name "Pantheon" comes from the Greek words meaning "all gods," reflecting its original purpose as a temple dedicated to all the Roman deities. Its most striking feature is its massive dome, which remains the largest unreinforced concrete dome in the world. At its center is the oculus, a circular opening about 27 feet in diameter that serves as the building's only source of natural light and ventilation — rain falls through it, draining through small holes in the sloped floor below.

The dome's design showcases extraordinary Roman engineering: its thickness decreases toward the top, and the concrete mixture used becomes progressively lighter with the addition of materials like pumice, reducing the overall weight and structural stress.

In 609 AD, the Pantheon was consecrated as a Christian church, a conversion that is largely credited with its remarkable preservation compared to other ancient Roman structures, many of which were stripped for building materials or left to decay. It continues to function as a church today.

The Pantheon also serves as a burial site for notable figures, including the Renaissance painter Raphael and several Italian kings.

Today, it remains one of the most influential buildings in architectural history, inspiring countless structures including the US Capitol and numerous other domed buildings around the world.
